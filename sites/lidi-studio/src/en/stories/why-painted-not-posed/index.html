Why "Painted, Not Posed"

Three words on a homepage. Years of practice underneath.

I get asked, often, what the slogan means. "Painted, not posed" is short enough to fit on a card and slow enough to take a while to explain. Here's the long version.

Origin of the slogan

It came out of a frustration. For years I'd describe my work and watch people nod politely while reaching for the closest reference they had — wedding photographer, lifestyle photographer, family photographer. None of those quite fit what I do. The reference that actually fits is older and lives in a different building: the portrait painter. Someone who composes, layers, sits with the work. I started saying "painted, not posed" the way you say a thing once you've found it.

The difference between snapshots and portraits

A snapshot is a moment caught. It's reactive. It depends on the photographer being quick — quick enough to anticipate the smile, the gesture, the look. The work happens in fractions of a second.

"The camera, when it finally fires, is the last gesture in a sequence that started hours earlier."

A portrait, the way a painter would mean it, is composed. Wardrobe is chosen before anyone walks in. Light is shaped. Posture is considered, not directed. The body is honored — not asked to perform. The work happens slowly, and the camera, when it finally fires, is the last gesture in a sequence that started hours or days earlier.

Both can be beautiful. They are not the same thing.

How a session actually unfolds

If you book a session with me, very little happens fast. We start with a conversation — sometimes weeks before the date. I want to know what chapter you're in. I want to know whether you've been photographed before, and what you didn't like about it. I want to see the kind of light you respond to, the way you carry yourself when you're not performing.

By the time you arrive at the studio, the wardrobe has been considered. The light has been thought about. We talk again. We work slowly. I tell you what I'm seeing — I tell you almost nothing about what to do. The portrait is built around what's already true about you, not in spite of it.

What clients can expect

Slowness, mostly. The opposite of "say cheese." A photographer who will talk you through what's happening but won't direct you through it. A small number of finished portraits — composed, considered, finished — instead of a large number of shutter clicks. Something you'll want to live with for decades, rather than scroll past tomorrow.

That's what the slogan means. If it resonates, begin a conversation, or look through the portfolio to see whether the work feels right.
